# Thrifted Tease V2 Brand And Voice

## Purpose
This document expands the V2 plan's brand direction into practical writing and positioning guidance for future copy, layout decisions, and visual choices.

## Brand Core
Thrifted Tease should feel like thrift-store glamour with live-room credibility.

The important tension is:
- collected, not polished to sterility
- stylish, not precious
- romantic, not soft-focus
- local and human, not small-time
- mysterious, not vague

## Brand Promises
When someone lands on the site, the experience should quickly communicate:
- this is a real band with taste
- this band exists in actual rooms, not just online profiles
- the music world feels warm, textured, and slightly flirtatious
- the band has enough identity to be memorable without overselling itself

## Voice Principles

### 1. Sound like people, not layout notes
User-facing copy should never explain that something is a "cleaner page," a "focused section," or a "three-item layout."

### 2. Be specific without over-explaining
Good copy should imply the room, the mood, or the texture without becoming purple prose.

### 3. Keep the confidence grounded
The site should feel self-assured, but it should not inflate scale, prestige, or momentum.

### 4. Let the band feel slightly magnetic
The voice should invite people closer. It should not read like administration copy or artist-bio boilerplate.

## Voice Sliders

### More of this
- warm
- textured
- sly
- scene-aware
- invitational
- lived-in
- image-rich

### Less of this
- corporate
- generic premium
- procedural
- vague hype
- platform-marketing language
- product-demo phrasing

## Copy Patterns To Avoid
- "Official home"
- "Available on all platforms"
- "Focused layout"
- "Built for"
- "Cleaner record"
- "Designed to"
- "Future products can be added by duplicating"

These phrases may be useful in docs, but not in public-facing site copy.

## Copy Patterns To Prefer
- language about rooms, nights, chemistry, texture, and atmosphere
- concise lines that imply character
- short phrases that feel quotable or memorable
- headlines that sound chosen, not generated

## Messaging By Surface

### Homepage
Goal:
Make the band feel immediate before explaining anything.

Use:
- a strong headline or statement
- one supporting line that suggests sound, setting, or energy
- CTA labels that feel clear but not generic

Avoid:
- generic genre-plus-city labeling as the primary message
- subtitles that frame the site as an official portal

### Media
Goal:
Make the archive feel like evidence of a scene and a memory.

Use:
- venue-aware intros
- concise captions
- documentary energy

Avoid:
- folder-management language
- over-explaining what the viewer is seeing

### Shows
Goal:
Communicate motion, credibility, and invitation.

Use:
- venue and room energy
- local scene confidence
- honest framing when the calendar is light

Avoid:
- fake-tour framing
- copy that sounds inflated or defensive

### Merch
Goal:
Make merch feel like an extension of the band world.

Use:
- tactile language
- drop framing when true
- specific visual identity cues

Avoid:
- placeholder ecommerce phrasing
- generic product-card language

## Homepage Positioning Directions
Possible lanes to explore:
- vintage color, live chemistry, and late-night art-pop
- thrift-store glamour for real rooms
- local-room warmth with a sharper edge

These are directional lanes, not final headlines. Final copy should be shorter and more ownable than the phrases above.

## Naming And Labeling Guidance
- Prefer shorter nav labels where possible.
- Use page labels that reflect user intent, not internal structure.
- Consider whether "Media Gallery" should become something more editorial in V2, depending on final IA.

## Editorial Test
Before approving any V2 copy, ask:
- Could this line appear on any indie band site?
- Does it describe the website instead of the band?
- Does it sound more like process documentation than artist language?
- Does it feel too polished to belong to this world?

If the answer is yes, rewrite it.
